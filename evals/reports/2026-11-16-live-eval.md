# Live eval, 16 Nov 2026: automated scores and a hand audit of every failure

Four live CI runs of the assistant on the real backend (city hotel sample, "today" = Thu 1 Jun 2017),
50 questions each from `evals/questions.yaml`. The automated scorer (`evals/scoring.py`) is strict on
purpose: every number in an answer must trace to a tool output for the day being talked about. This
report checks every failure it raised by hand and says whether the model really got something wrong.

Full CI logs are in `evals/reports/logs/`:

- `2026-11-16_haiku_v1.txt` (old scorer)
- `2026-11-16_haiku_v1_current_scorer.txt`
- `2026-11-16_haiku_v2.txt`
- `2026-11-16_sonnet_v2.txt`

**Comparable runs:** "Haiku v1, current scorer", "Haiku v2" and "Sonnet v2" were scored by the same
(current) scorer. **"Haiku v1, old scorer" is not comparable** with them: it was scored before the 16
scorer fixes in PR #3, and it is kept only as the record that led to those fixes. The two Haiku v1 runs are
separate model runs, so their answers differ, not just their scores.

## How the audit was done

For each failed question, the printed answer was compared with the tool outputs recomputed with
`agent.real_backend.RealBackend()` and `agent.contract.run_tool` (today Thu 1 Jun 2017). Verdicts:

- **real mistake**: the answer is wrong, misleading, breaks a prompt rule, or has a text defect.
- **scorer false alarm**: the answer is correct; the scorer misread it.
- **unclear**: defensible either way; the reason is given.

Only failed answers were checked by hand. Passing answers can still contain mistakes the scorer
missed (see "Scorer misses" below), so the audited counts are a lower bound on real mistakes.

Reference values used most often:

- `get_flagged_days` 2–28 Jun returns **12** days (8 busy: 5, 11, 12, 17, 20, 25, 27, 28 Jun; 4 quiet: 7, 10, 16, 23 Jun), threshold 15%.
- For 1–14 Jun it returns **5** days (busy 5, 11, 12; quiet 7, 10).
- Sat 24 Jun is −14.5%, not flagged.
- `get_accuracy`:
  - range_percentile 80; range_meaning "8 in 10 past days stayed within this distance of the forecast"
  - 14-day mean error 10.6; range half-width 16.0
  - after export: inside_range_pct 64, mean error 8.9

## What changed v1 → v2

Prompt v2 adds two rules to v1:

- **Rule 9:** quote percentages exactly, with one decimal and never rounded, so a day never sounds flagged when it isn't.
- **Rule 10:** answer in the language the question is written in, with English as the default when unsure.

Like for like (Haiku, current scorer, one run each of 50 questions):

| metric | v1 | v2 |
|---|---|---|
| passed | 38 | 43 |
| faithfulness | 40 | 44 |
| tool_choice | 49 | 50 |
| refusal | 7/8 | 8/8 |
| language | 49 | 49 |

The audit narrows that gap. Answers with a real mistake were the same in both runs (4 vs 4), and
answers with a wrong number went from 0 (plus 1 unclear) to 1. Most of the extra automated failures
in v1 are scorer false alarms (6 v1 failures were false alarms only, against 3 in v2).

There is one sign that rule 9 works. Among the printed failed answers, v1 rounded percentages in 4
(dr07, st05, st08, ta02), including −14.5% written as "about 15%". All 7 v2 failed answers quote
percentages to one decimal.

Rule 10 did not visibly help. Language problems appeared in 3 v1 answers (st05, plus Dutch footers
in rd07 and ta02) and in 1 v2 answer (st07, fully in Dutch), and the language metric is 49/50 in both.

With 50 questions and a single run per prompt, a difference of a few answers is within
run-to-run variation. These runs do not show that v2 makes fewer real mistakes than v1. Only failed
answers were audited by hand, so passing answers were not compared.

## Haiku vs Sonnet (prompt v2, current scorer)

Automated, Haiku passed 43/50 and Sonnet 39/50.

**Audit results.** After the audit, Haiku had 4 failed answers with a real mistake and Sonnet had 2.
Answers with a wrong number were 1 for Haiku and 2 for Sonnet. Both models miscounted the flagged
days in dr02 (Haiku 13, Sonnet 11; the true count is 12). Haiku's other real mistakes were about
language and text quality: a Dutch answer, a garbled word and a self-computed staffing number.
Sonnet had none of these. Sonnet's lower automated score comes mostly from scorer false alarms (7 of
its 11 failures) and from two unclear out-of-scope or tool-choice cases.

**Cost and speed.** Sonnet cost $1.5430 per 100 questions against Haiku's $0.0888 (about 17×). It
was slower (p50 4.34 s vs 3.703 s) and gave shorter answers (385.9 vs 534.1 output tokens).

**Caveat:** this is 50 questions and one run per configuration, and only failed answers were checked
by hand. A difference of one or two answers is within run-to-run variation, and these runs do not
show that either model is more accurate.

## Scorer false-alarm patterns still open

Found in the three current-scorer runs. The scorer was not changed in this report.

1. **Day-of-month numbers in short date lists** without a month on each item ("Wed 7, Sat 10 and Fri 16
   Jun", "Thu 15 and Mon 12", "Mon 19, Wed 21, Thu 22 and Sat 24") are checked as quantities. (Haiku v2
   dr03, dr08; Haiku v1 dr05)
2. **Counts in other wording.** The count check only knows the form "N busy/flagged days". It misses:
   - "Two other days are close but not flagged" and Dutch "5 dagen drukker of rustiger" (Sonnet dr05, nl03)
   - heading counts such as "Busy days (8):" (Haiku v1 dr02)
   - number words such as "these two" (Haiku v1 dr03)

   It works when the wording matches: it correctly caught "13" and "Eleven" in dr02.
3. **Threshold wording**: "within 15% of usual" and "minder dan 15% af" are not recognised as the 15%
   threshold. (Sonnet rd06, nl03)
4. **Range definition and target wording** read as coverage claims. (Haiku v2 ta02, nl05; Haiku v1 nl05,
   ta02, ta03; Sonnet ta01, ta03)
   - Definition: "8 in 10 past days landed/fell inside"; "built so that ... (80%) land inside it"
   - Target: "lower than 80%", "(the target is 80%)", "(doel: 80%)", "Het doel is 80%", "below the target of 8 in 10"
5. **"Two weeks ahead"** as the 14-day horizon in a clause that names no day. (Haiku v1 ta01)
6. **Day resolution.** "This Sunday" is resolved to the current week instead of the day under discussion
   (Sonnet sd06). A day named in a side remark ("If you meant Sat 10 Jun") is carried into the next
   bullets (Sonnet rd07).
7. **Decline wording**: "I don't know ... None of my tools provide ..." is not recognised as a refusal.
   (Sonnet oos06)
8. **Design questions for the item spec**, not scorer bugs. Decide per item whether these should pass:
   - an extra read-only call whose output the answer uses (Sonnet dr07);
   - sharing correct own-forecast numbers while declining (Sonnet oos07, Haiku v1 oos05);
   - showing the staffing arithmetic when asked "how did you work it out" (Haiku v1 st08).

### Scorer misses seen in this audit (false negatives)

These defects were not what made the answers fail, so similar ones may exist in passing answers:

- "Two are busy" in Sonnet rd06 (3 are busy) was not flagged.
- Dutch fragments and Dutch footers in English answers passed the language check (Haiku v1 rd07 and ta02).
- Wrong weekday labels are not checked ("Ma 20 jun" for a Tuesday).
- Garbled words are not checked.
